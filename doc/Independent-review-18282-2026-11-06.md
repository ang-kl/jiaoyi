## §3. Class-by-Class Bottleneck Diagnosis (`doc/AI-review-prompt-tested-18280-2026-11-06.md` §5)

  - **Cost & Admission**: On `2025-08-12`, `SpotBrent`'s first-hour range $R$ was smaller than the `24bp/side` (`48bp` + spread) cost reserve $C$, triggering a `cost_veto`.
  - **Execution Path**: On `2025-08-05`, both `SpotBrent` (`+0.4283R`) and `SpotCrude` (`+0.2244R`) survived the 30-second Chandelier trail and closed profitable at the 4-hour session end, even after deducting `24bp/side` (`48bp` round-trip), but neither reached `TP1 = 1.38R + C`.

### §3.2. Metals (`XAUUSD`, `XAGUSD`)
- **Primary Bottleneck**: **(3) Entry economically infeasible under the frozen `24bp/side` (`48bp` round-trip) reserve $C \ge R$ gate (`0` admitted trades)**.

## §4. Methodological Critique (`doc/AI-assessment-and-next-plan-18281-2026-11-06.md` §8.3 & §8.4)

### §4.1. Why Lowering Payout Slippage (`2bp` / `3.74bp`) While Keeping `24bp` Admission/Target Geometry Is Incomplete (`§8.3`)
- Codex correctly notes in `№18,281` `§3` that the `2bp` and `3.74bp` columns in `№18,280` **only adjust the post-exit payout deduction** while holding the **`24bp/side` (`48bp` round-trip) admission gate ($C < R$) and target distance ($\text{TP1} = 1.38R + C$) fixed**.
- **Three distinct consequences follow from that separation**:
  1. **For Indices**: Holding entries fixed and lowering payout drag to `2bp/side` is **sufficient to falsify the current index entry/exit combination**, because even on the 6 widest-range index episodes ($R > C_{24\text{bp}}$) with `2bp/side` exit drag, expectancy is negative (`-0.0501R`, `PF = 0.80`) and inferior to `always_long` (`-0.1907R`).
  2. **For Metals**: Lowering payout drag to `2bp/side` while keeping the `24bp/side` admission gate ($C_{24\text{bp}} < R$) **tests nothing at all**, because `0` trades are admitted!
  3. **For Target Geometry (`TP1 = 1.38R + C`) Across All Classes**: When $C$ is inflated by `48 bps` (`C_{24\text{bp}}`), and the admission gate allows any trade with $R > C_{24\text{bp}}$ (say $C_{24\text{bp}} = 0.8R$), `TP1` is pushed from `1.38R + C_{\text{actual}}` (`~1.5R`) out to **`1.38R + 0.8R = 2.18R`**. That explains why **not a single trade (`0/20`) across any asset class hit `TP1` in `№18,280`**!

### §4.2. Critique of Codex's Proposed 20-Episode Oil Batch (`21 Jul – 11 Aug 2025`, `§6.C` & `§8.4`)
- **What Is Good in Codex's Proposal**:
  - The 10 proposed dates (`21, 23, 25, 28, 30 July` and `1, 4, 6, 8, 11 August 2025` for `SpotCrude` and `SpotBrent` = `20` episodes across 4 calendar weeks) are all prior to the reserved validation window (`19 Aug – 5 Sep 2025`), exclude the two already-inspected development dates (`5` and `12 Aug 2025`), and fall on regular Monday/Wednesday/Friday trading days (no US/UK oil market holidays fall on those 10 dates; the UK Summer Bank Holiday is `25 Aug 2025`).
  - Requiring $\ge 5$ closed scenarios per oil symbol across $\ge 3$ distinct calendar weeks before opening validation is statistically sound.
- **Two Flaws to Fix in Codex's Oil Proposal Before Running Collection**:
  1. **Selection Bias / Post-5-August Clustering**: Notice that `5 August 2025` (the winning date in `№18,280`) sits right in the middle of `4, 6, 8, 11 August 2025`! Moreover, why were `5` and `12 August 2025` (two Tuesdays) chosen originally? Because the original development dates were **Tuesdays**. Sampling Mon/Wed/Fri around an already-known winning Tuesday (`5 August`) mixes day-of-week effects and overlaps the exact macro week (`4–8 August 2025`) already inspected across 20 symbols. Even more importantly, verify in `crates/jiaoyi/src/strategy3.rs` (`BAR_DAYS = 45`, line 103) that the `5` and `12 August` M30 sigma-table preparation bars did not already expose the `07:00–11:00 UTC` M30 bars of `21 Jul – 11 Aug 2025` to prior inspection.
  2. **Expected Yield Under the `24bp/side` Gate (`n = 5` Closes/Symbol May Be Unreachable in 10 Dates)**: On `5` and `12 August`, only `1 of 2` (`50%`) oil dates passed the `24bp/side` cost gate ($C_{24\text{bp}} < R$) because `07:00–08:00 UTC` (London morning, before the `13:00–14:30 UTC` NYMEX crude pit open) frequently has a first-hour oil range $R < 55\text{ bps}$. If `50%` of `07:00 UTC` oil episodes are vetoed by `C_{24\text{bp}} >= R`, 10 dates will yield only `~5` closes per symbol (barely touching the minimum of `5`, with zero margin for flat predictors or censored closes).
